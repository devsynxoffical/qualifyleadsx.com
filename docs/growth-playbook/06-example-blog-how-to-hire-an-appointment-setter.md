# How to Hire an Appointment Setter (2026 Guide)

To hire an appointment setter, first decide what the setter will own: inbound follow-up, outbound outreach, or both. Then write a scorecard with measurable targets (speed to lead, booking rate, show rate) and test candidates with a live role-play. Pay a mix of base and per-show commission, and give them a script, a CRM and a 2-week ramp.

## What does an appointment setter actually do?

An appointment setter turns leads into booked, qualified sales calls. In a high-ticket business, that means:

- Replying to new leads within minutes by phone, SMS, WhatsApp or DM
- Asking qualifying questions about budget, timeline, decision-maker status and fit
- Booking qualified leads into the closer's calendar
- Confirming and reminding, so booked calls actually happen
- Logging everything in the CRM

A setter is not a closer. Setters protect the closer's time, and closers sell. See [outsourced sales closers](/outsourced-sales-closers) for the other half of the team.

## Step 1: Write a scorecard before the job ad

List the 3 to 5 numbers the setter will be judged on:

- **Speed to lead:** first reply in under 5 minutes during working hours
- **Contact rate:** share of new leads reached
- **Booking rate:** share of contacted leads booked
- **Show rate:** share of booked calls that happen. The cross-industry average is 58.2% with email-only confirmations, and much higher with fast SMS and WhatsApp follow-up ([show rate benchmarks](/benchmarks/show-rate)).
- **Qualified rate:** share of held calls the closer marks as qualified

## Step 2: Write the job description

Include the offer and price point, the lead sources, the hours, the tools (CRM, dialer, WhatsApp) and the pay plan. Be specific: "Call inbound leads from Meta ads for a $5,000 coaching program within 5 minutes" attracts the right people. "Sales role, high earning potential" does not.

## Step 4: Interview with a role-play

Skip the long interview. Run a 10-minute role-play: you play a lead who filled in the form but is hesitant about price. Score the candidate on:

1. Did they reply with energy and use the lead's name?
2. Did they ask about the problem before the price?
3. Did they qualify budget and decision-maker status without sounding scripted?
4. Did they book a specific time, or just "send a link"?

## Step 5: Set the pay plan

Common models are base plus per-booked-call, base plus per-show, and commission on closed deals. Paying per show, not per booking, stops calendars filling with no-shows. We compare the models in [appointment setter compensation models](/blog/appointment-setter-compensation-models).

## Step 6: Ramp them for two weeks

- Week 1: shadow calls, learn the offer, practise the script, and book with supervision
- Week 2: own a share of inbound leads, with daily call reviews
- Then: weekly scorecard reviews

## FAQ

**How many leads can one appointment setter handle?**
It depends on the channel and lead quality. A full-time inbound setter with AI-assisted follow-up can work [fill in] new leads a month. Track speed to lead: if it slips past 5 minutes, you need more capacity.

**Should an appointment setter be commission only?**
Commission-only setters tend to leave when leads are slow and to book anyone when they are fast. A small base plus a per-show bonus keeps them steady and honest.

**What skills matter most in an appointment setter?**
Speed, curiosity and discipline in the CRM. Charisma helps, but a setter who replies in 2 minutes and logs every lead beats a charming setter who replies tomorrow.

**Can AI replace appointment setters?**
AI handles the first reply, reminders and simple qualification well, and it never sleeps. People still book more high-ticket calls when a lead has questions. Most of our clients use both. See [speed to lead](/speed-to-lead).
